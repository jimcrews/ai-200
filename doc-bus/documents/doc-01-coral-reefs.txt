Coral Reefs Under Pressure

Coral reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, yet they support roughly a quarter of all marine species. Reefs are built by tiny animals called coral polyps, which live in partnership with algae that give them food and colour.

When ocean temperatures stay too high for too long, the polyps expel the algae and turn white. This is known as coral bleaching. Bleached coral is not dead, but it is starving, and if the heat does not ease within a few weeks, large sections of the reef can die.

Scientists are testing several ways to help reefs recover, including breeding heat-tolerant corals, replanting fragments grown in nurseries, and reducing local pressures such as pollution and overfishing. Most researchers agree, however, that the long-term survival of reefs depends on limiting further ocean warming.

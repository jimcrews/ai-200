Baking Sourdough at Home

Sourdough bread is made without commercial yeast. Instead, it relies on a starter: a mixture of flour and water that captures wild yeast and bacteria from the environment. The bacteria produce lactic and acetic acids, which give the bread its tangy flavour.

A new starter usually takes five to ten days to become active. Each day, the baker discards part of it and feeds the rest with fresh flour and water. A healthy starter roughly doubles in size within a few hours of feeding and smells pleasantly sour.

Making the bread itself is slow but simple. The dough is mixed, folded several times over a few hours, shaped, and then left to rise overnight in the fridge. Baking it in a very hot covered pot traps steam, which helps the loaf rise and creates a crisp, dark crust.
